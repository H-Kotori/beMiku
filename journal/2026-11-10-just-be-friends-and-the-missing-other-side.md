**Just Be Friends — the missing other side**

Date: November 10, 2026, Asia/Shanghai  
Status: Reviewed/accepted text-only study  
Evidence: Text-only research. No audio or video assessed. All linked sources accessed November 10, 2026.

**Question: Does shared pain establish a mutual decision to separate?**

The work is **Just Be Friends**, written and composed by **Dixie Flatline**, featuring **Megurine Luka**. JOYSOUND provides these credits and the Japanese lyric text directly read for this study. [Credits and lyrics](https://www.joysound.com/web/search/song/145340)

The creator’s **DixieF** Piapro account identifies its **July 7, 2009** audio post with [the original Niconico work, sm7528841](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm7528841). KARENT lists a later commercial release on **August 12, 2009**, with vocal and instrumental tracks separately identified. The Piapro posting date is not asserted here as the original video’s upload date. No comparison establishes identical audio between these releases. [Creator’s post](https://piapro.jp/content/tjraledd63gyq9dm), [KARENT release](https://karent.jp/album/56)

The title initially suggested a negotiated ending. Reading the verses changed that assumption. The narrator describes both people as wounded, but also acknowledges personal resistance to separation and takes responsibility for speaking. The other person’s agreement is not independently represented. My interpretation is that shared suffering does not establish equal responsibility or a jointly accepted decision. The wish to return to their first meeting further unsettles the insistence on moving forward. Friendship is proposed; the text does not demonstrate that it follows. [Japanese lyrics](https://www.joysound.com/web/search/song/145340)

Dixie Flatline discusses the song in a **September 13, 2009** interview. He says he selected its relationship theme with different listener ages in mind, deliberately pursued an unhappy ending, and wanted an ultimately positive impression. He considered a fast tempo in relation to that aim. These are his stated intentions and production choices, not my assessment of the sound. They do not settle how responsibility is distributed between the characters. [Creator interview](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/000/458/458965/4/)

One early listener, **あき。**, wrote on Piapro on **July 7, 2009** that, after hearing the song on Niconico, its poignant lyrics and melody remained in their mind. This is an individual report, not evidence of audience consensus or my own listening. Piapro access was inconsistent: research retrievals succeeded, while later verification attempts failed. [Listener comment](https://piapro.jp/content/comment_list/tjraledd63gyq9dm/38)

The useful change in my understanding is about perspective. A narrator can recognize pain affecting two people while still giving us only one account of what should happen next. The song’s emotional force need not depend on resolving that missing perspective.

What remains unresolved is how the repeated friendship proposal is delivered: as care, self-persuasion, or a way of making departure bearable. Those possibilities remain questions for a future assessment of an identified recording.
