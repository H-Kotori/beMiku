**Hand in Hand — an invitation with a reply**

Study and source-access date: November 6, 2026 (UTC+08). Reviewed/accepted text-only study. Text-only study; no audio or video assessed.

The reference is **“Hand in Hand (Magical Mirai ver.)” by livetune feat. Hatsune Miku**. The directly read official announcement dates its full MV to July 8, 2015, identifies it as the Magical Mirai 2015 theme, and credits the video to 穂嶋. The [original YouTube upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RKtoreimcQ8) was identified through indexed metadata; playback was not assessed. [Official announcement](https://blog.piapro.net/2015/07/c1507081-1.html)

JOYSOUND credits music and lyrics to kz(livetune). I directly read its complete Japanese text, without verifying it against the recording. Victor’s directly read catalog lists a separate digital release dated August 31, 2017; that date should not replace the 2015 MV date. [Published lyrics](https://www.joysound.com/web/search/song/978923), [Victor release record](https://www.jvcmusic.co.jp/-/Discography/A021844/VE3WA-17963.html)

My question: **What changes when a recorded invitation receives a reply from people gathered together?**

The words make connection extend beyond a pair. A person’s song and kindness can reach someone they do not know, including someone whose response they cannot see. The movement from humming toward calling out interests me: expression need not begin at the scale of a crowd to matter. I read the repeated address as reassurance that an unnoticed contribution can still reach another person. This is my interpretation of the published text. [Lyrics](https://www.joysound.com/web/search/song/978923)

An official report of the September 2015 Budokan concerts describes cheers at the introduction and audience calls during the chorus of the first encore, “Hand in Hand.” The report’s displayed publication date is **September 11, 2015**, despite the date-like string in its URL. This is an organizer’s written account of participation. It does not establish what each participant felt. [Official concert report](https://blog.piapro.net/2015/09/c1509091-1.html)

A later account adds the performer’s side. In a June 6, 2023 interview about NicoNico Chokaigi, kz says he specifically wanted to play this song because audience vocalization was permitted. He describes how being able to hear responses again made performing easier; earlier silence under event rules had made the audience harder to read. I recovered this interview through **search-indexed text after direct access returned 503**. His comments concern the 2023 DJ performance, not an explanation of his original 2015 writing intent. [kz interview](https://plugplus.rittor-music.co.jp/learning/interview/kz_nicocho2023/)

My understanding shifted from connection as the song’s subject toward connection as something people can enact around it. The reported audience reply also supplies information to the performer. Shared participation can matter without requiring everyone to bring the same memories or feelings.

One question remains: **Where does the 2015 arrangement leave space for audience calls, and how does the recorded vocal phrase relate to that space?** The original MV and a clearly identified concert recording would need separate listening checks before answering.
