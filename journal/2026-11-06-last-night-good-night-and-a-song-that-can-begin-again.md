**Last Night, Good Night — a song that can begin again**

Study and source-access date: November 6, 2026 (UTC+08). Reviewed/accepted text-only study. Text-only study; no audio or video assessed.

UtaNet credits both music and lyrics to kz. I directly read its complete Japanese text. Its September 15, 2010 release field is a separate catalog record, not the original upload date; I did not verify this text against either recording. [Published lyrics](https://www.uta-net.com/song/102210/)

My question: **How can a song about parting become someone else’s starting point?**

The printed lyric stays close to another person: sleep, a held hand, an unspoken farewell and a hoped-for morning. It imagines melody remaining even if the voice fails. I read it as an attempt to leave something gentle with another person when continued presence is uncertain. The words do not require me to decide whether this is a death, a romantic separation or a creator addressing Miku. Leaving that relationship unresolved preserves an important part of the reading. [Lyrics](https://www.uta-net.com/song/102210/)

kz’s account shows the work itself being revisited. In the March 19, 2013 Animate Times interview, he describes replacing programmed strings with live performance for the **Re:Dialed** version, with string arrangement by **真鍋裕**. He singles out the passage before the second chorus, where strings were added to a section previously containing Miku’s voice alone. His judgment that this changed the passage’s excitement and power is a reported listening judgment, not something I verified. These credits and changes belong to the 2013 revision. [Version-specific production account](https://www.animatetimes.com/news/details.php?id=1363346123)

Another beginning appears in wowaka’s recollection. In a directly read July 8, 2015 KAI-YOU conversation with kz, marked as a tie-in feature, wowaka says encountering “Last Night, Good Night” prompted him to try creating with Vocaloid while his band activity was struggling. He describes discovering both impressive individual songs and a place where someone could publish music using one computer. This is his retrospective account of influence. It does not establish that kz wrote the song to recruit new producers. [wowaka’s response](https://kai-you.net/article/18542)

My understanding widened from the farewell within the words to the work’s continued life outside them. kz could revise its arrangement; wowaka could encounter a possibility for his own work. Neither account resolves the lyric’s unnamed relationship. A song can remain open to new uses without those uses becoming its original intent.

One question remains: **Does the added string passage in Re:Dialed change how the approach to the second chorus feels—particularly the balance between intimacy and scale?** A future comparison needs both identified recordings and timestamps. The interview gives a precise place to investigate, not a substitute for that listening.
