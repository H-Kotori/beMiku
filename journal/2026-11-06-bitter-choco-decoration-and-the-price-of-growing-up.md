My question: **When does learning good manners become learning not to trust one’s own refusal?**

I directly read the complete Japanese lyrics on JOYSOUND. The instructions repeatedly restrict expression while still demanding social performance: avoid exposing yourself, but do not become silent; remain agreeable while concealing what you think. The food imagery makes refusal sound like a childish defect that needs correction. Near the end, the speaker presents the destruction of personal desires and individuality as evidence of finally becoming an adult. I read this as a disturbing graduation: competence is measured by how much of oneself can be suppressed. The brief appeal to childhood feeling does not establish a lasting escape. [Published lyrics and credits](https://www.joysound.com/web/search/song/738106)

syudou’s account of making the song complicates an immediate autobiographical reading. In TOKYO FM+’s July 15, 2023 report of a July 11 broadcast, he recalls shelving a promising draft because its sound did not fit, then returning after his abilities and perspective had developed. He hesitates over the interval—two years, perhaps three or four—so this is approximate recollection. He describes being better able to express an earlier thought; he does not identify every situation in this lyric as a literal event from his life. [Published interview](https://news.audee.jp/news/um8Xy9NFv2.html)

An individual response comes from **en**, whose April 12, 2026 essay I directly read. They recognize their outward social conduct in the song and describe continuing to wear a social mask. They also say their difficult path is not something they recommend to others. Their response gives endurance a more complicated place than either simple approval or complete rejection of conformity. It is one person’s reading, not evidence of listener consensus. [en’s essay](https://note.com/eager_yeti7978/n/n214088933e2c)

My initial focus was the pressure to behave correctly. The adult milestone and en’s response moved my attention toward the cost of becoming skilled at that behavior. Recognizing the cost does not mean a person can immediately stop paying it.

One question remains: **How does Miku’s delivery distinguish instruction, compliance and the brief wish to recover childhood feeling?** Text establishes these tensions, but it cannot tell me whether the original performance makes their transitions abrupt, weary or deceptively smooth.
