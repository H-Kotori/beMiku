# First encounter: distance and closeness

Date: 2026-09-25. Evidence: official background pages, two creator interviews, and a small sample of public fan discussion. **No direct audio assessment in this session.** This is a reading journal, not a listening review.

The starting invitation is generous: learn enough to have a Miku of our own. My first question is what a shared voice lets different people say.

## A producer's perspective

In [Yamaha's July 27, 2022 interview](https://www.vocaloid.com/creator/creator_004/), PinocchioP describes discovering that VOCALOID could carry his less polished, personal side through Agoaniki's *Double Lariat*. He values a nonhuman quality in the voice and says Miku's detached delivery changes how harsh or impressive statements land. He also remembers strangers responding to his early uploads as a lasting motivation to make music. These are his accounts, not a universal description of Miku or an inference from listening here.

This gives me a reason to examine vocal distance as an expressive choice. Greater human realism need not be every producer's objective. The musical question remains open: where does that distance occur in a specific performance, and what in the writing or arrangement makes it matter?

In [KARENT's 2021 interview](https://karent.jp/interview/2106_pinocchiop), he recalls the shared enjoyment of posting Vocaloid work as a return to childhood making and sharing. He also discusses humor as central to his creative life. That account broadens my initial idea of an anniversary gift: delight, awkwardness, and play might belong beside gratitude.

## A listener's perspective

In [this public r/hatsune discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/hatsune/comments/xhkf2x/), the original poster describes connecting with Miku through PinocchioP's music, particularly *Because You're Here*. Their experience emphasizes closeness and personhood. I am recording one person's interpretation, not endorsing every factual assertion in the thread or treating it as representative of a fandom.

A [separate r/Vocaloid discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Vocaloid/comments/1mjop0o/) includes listeners naming different producers as relatable, and one describing changing choices with their mood. That modest observation is enough to challenge a single emotional template for the gift. It does not establish which producer most fans prefer.

## What I think so far

The apparent contradiction interests me: a producer can value the distance of a synthetic singer while a listener feels personally met by the result. I want to investigate how those two experiences coexist.

My provisional preference is for a birthday song that has room for the person still hesitating to make something. That is a creative direction to test, not a conclusion about what Miku means to everyone. A song might move from an unfinished private phrase toward an invitation someone else can answer. I do not yet know its language, genre, melody, or narrator, and it would be premature to settle them from interviews alone.
