**God-ish — the critic is inside the picture**

Study and source-access date: November 5, 2026 (UTC+08). Reviewed/accepted text-only study. Text-only study; no audio or video assessed.

The reference is PinocchioP’s original **神っぽいな / God-ish**, announced September 17, 2021. His official page credits him with music, lyrics, video and illustration; ヤヅキ with **“Acoustic guitar (R)”**; and Hatsune Miku with vocals. It links the [original YouTube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHBFKhLUVig) and [Niconico upload](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm39334235). Both playback routes were inaccessible. The [official announcement](https://pinocchiop.com/news/795), interviews and [complete Japanese lyric page](https://www.uta-net.com/song/347616/) were directly read. UtaNet’s May 17, 2023 release field belongs to its later release record.

My question: **When a song judges people, does it give its own judging voice a privileged position?**

The printed words repeatedly move between attraction and contempt. They scrutinize appearance, opinions, cultural products and claims to insight. A passage explicitly frames criticism as self-admonition, while the ending introduces a wish for less burdened enjoyment. My reading is that the desire to stand above the scene becomes part of what the song examines. Recognizing shallowness does not automatically make the recognizer wise. [Published lyrics](https://www.uta-net.com/song/347616/)

PinocchioP’s account helps resist an easy reading of the song as his personal attack on casual listeners. In a May 24, 2023 PR interview, he describes observing a cycle of aggressive songs, criticism and further observers. He says the apparently enlightened viewpoint is imperfect, and that the unpleasant judgments voiced in the song are not automatically his own beliefs. These are his stated intentions; they cannot dictate how every listener experiences the words. [RealSound interview](https://realsound.jp/2023/05/post-1333325.html)

A different response comes from DECO*27 in a March 12, 2022 PR conversation. He praises the song’s memorable chorus and fit between sounds, alongside its broad view of phenomena. He also describes feeling challenged by its quality. Those are his listening judgments, not sonic properties I verified. His account interests me because pleasure and thoughtful attention coexist within it. [DECO*27’s response](https://realsound.jp/2022/03/post-983704.html)

My initial expectation was a satire aimed outward at supposedly foolish people. The creator’s explanation and the text changed my focus toward the unstable position of the observer—including me when I interpret the song. Understanding its references would not place me outside its subject.

One question remains: **What in Miku’s delivery distinguishes a judgment being enacted from a judgment being endorsed?** Changes in emphasis might matter, but I have no listening evidence for them yet. The original video identified above remains the appropriate reference.
