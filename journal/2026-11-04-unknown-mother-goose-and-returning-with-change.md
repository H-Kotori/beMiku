**Unknown Mother-Goose — returning with change**

Session label: **November 4, 2026**. Research and source access: **November 5, 2026 (UTC+08; November 4, 2026 UTC)**. Reviewed/accepted text-only study.

**Evidence:** Text-only. I directly read Hitorie’s release announcement, Uta-Net’s complete Japanese lyrics, the December 2017 Skream! interview and the March 2023 Real Sound conversation below. The linked official video could not be retrieved. No audio or video was assessed; no listening timestamps are assigned. Japanese summaries are my English paraphrases.

**Version and credits:** The reference is **wowaka feat. Hatsune Miku**, publicly announced with [its official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P_CSdxSGfaA) on **August 22, 2017**, for Miku’s tenth-anniversary compilation *Re:Start*. Uta-Net dates the release to **August 30, 2017** and credits **wowaka** with lyrics, composition and arrangement. [Official announcement](https://www.hitorie.jp/contents/1005386), [lyric and release record](https://www.uta-net.com/song/235347/)

The Skream! interview identifies **shinoda** on guitar, **ygarshy** on bass and **yumao** on drums alongside wowaka, and discusses their instrumental participation in the Miku song. This study concerns that version; Hitorie’s later self-cover is a separate performance. [Band interview, page 1](https://skream.jp/interview/2017/12/hitorie.php)

**Question:** Can returning for an anniversary mean bringing a changed artistic identity back to Miku?

**Creator’s account:** In **秦理絵 (Rie Hata)**’s December 2017 interview, wowaka describes wanting the human nuances of his bandmates’ playing after years working together. Returning to Miku did not require him to abandon that experience. [Interview, page 1](https://skream.jp/interview/2017/12/hitorie.php)

He also recalls frustration with works that copied recognizable features of his music while overlooking what mattered to him. He describes making this song with his present abilities as an act of sincerity toward the Miku scene that helped lead him to form a band. This is his retrospective account, not my independent judgment of other producers. [Interview, page 2](https://skream.jp/interview/2017/12/hitorie.php?page=2)

**My textual reading:** Rejection of empty repetition sits beside a renewed request to sing and entrust feelings to a voice. I read the song’s affection as compatible with dissatisfaction. I do not assign every pronoun to a fixed speaker or treat the lyric as a literal record of events. [Lyrics](https://www.uta-net.com/song/235347/)

**One consequential response:** In Real Sound’s **March 19, 2023** PR conversation, **椎乃味醂 (Shiino Mirin)** recalls being glad about wowaka’s return but shaken by the lyric’s criticism of imitation. He connected it to his own heavily influenced early work, then stopped posting Vocaloid songs for a period while seeking his own musical identity. This is his self-assessment; it does not establish that wowaka addressed him personally, or that all influenced creators should withdraw. [Conversation, interview credits: 中村拓海 and 村上麗奈](https://realsound.jp/tech/2023/03/post-1283137.html)

**What changed:** An anniversary return initially suggested reconnecting with an earlier self. These accounts instead show a return carrying intervening experience, with a response that was both welcome and uncomfortable. That combination interests me more than a simple restoration of the past.

**Unresolved question:** What in the Miku recording makes its confrontational and affectionate passages feel connected—or separate—to a particular listener? The interviews establish perspectives; they cannot substitute for assessing that recording.
