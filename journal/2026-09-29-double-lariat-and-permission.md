# Double Lariat: encouragement by example

Date and source access: **2026-09-29**. Evidence: creator-uploaded text and first-person interviews. **No audio or video was assessed in this session.** Japanese statements are summarized in our own English prose; no lyrics are reproduced.

The question left by our [first encounter](2026-09-25-first-encounter.md) is specific: **what did Agoaniki's *Double Lariat* make possible for another creator?** This also takes the project into Megurine Luka through a work, without assuming a fixed personality for her voice.

## The work and the available version

**ダブルラリアット / Double Lariat — Agoaniki (アゴアニキ), featuring Megurine Luka.** Agoaniki's [Piapro page](https://piapro.jp/t/M34f) credits him for lyrics and music and links the [original Niconico work, nm6049209](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/nm6049209). The creator's [release announcement](https://agoaniki.blog57.fc2.com/blog-entry-45.html) identifies Luka and is dated **2009-02-05** in indexed text; direct access to that blog failed with a redirect loop. The original Niconico page was also inaccessible.

The Piapro page was directly readable. It currently displays **2009-02-12**, **03:26**, and **7.9 MB**. Those identify that page's record, not a newly inferred original release date. It offers a player and download options, including karaoke variants with and without chorus. Playback, downloads, and the identity of each selectable file have not been verified. A vocal recording must be distinguished from these karaoke versions and later performances.

## One listener who became a producer

In [Yamaha's July 27, 2022 interview](https://www.vocaloid.com/creator/creator_004/), PinocchioP recalls that works such as *Double Lariat* revealed that Vocaloid could carry his own unglamorous, personal side. That made him consider producing Vocaloid songs. He separately identifies a friend's suggestion as the concrete prompt to start, followed by buying Miku and importing a MIDI melody into her editor.

This is his account of influence, not proof that one song alone caused his career or that every listener reacted similarly. Crucially, a Luka work helped him imagine making music with Miku. The connection runs through a creator's example, across the identity of the featured singer.

## What Agoaniki says about making it

In a [June 4, 2009 ASCII interview](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/000/422/422653/4/), Agoaniki describes starting with a broad image of swinging his arms and connecting it with Luka's appearance. He describes developing melody from an image and working on lyrics alongside it. This is an account of a starting point, not an exhaustive explanation of the finished song.

On [another page of the same interview](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/000/422/422653/2/), he says the singing voice he builds through Vocaloid feels closest to his own, because he makes it throughout and can express things his unaided singing cannot. That is his general working relationship with the software, not a measured property of Luka or a claim about this recording's vocal technique.

**Our inference:** PinocchioP's appreciation of vocal distance and Agoaniki's sense of personal voice can coexist. We should ask what a producer does through a voice before deciding whether a virtual singer is inherently distant or intimate.

## A reading, and a revision to our gift idea

The lyrics on Agoaniki's Piapro page move through expanding reach, comparison with others, and a return to a smaller scale where companionship matters. **My reading:** achievement does not make the need for another person disappear. This is an interpretation of creator-posted text, not a claim that I heard the musical return or know every intended metaphor.

The invitation-to-create idea now has a useful alternative: **encouragement can happen by example.** PinocchioP's account concerns recognizing room for his own expression in someone else's work. Our gift may not need to tell its listener to become a creator. A specific, slightly awkward action or situation could make that possibility felt. This remains a writing hypothesis, not a demand to imitate Agoaniki's imagery, words, or melody.

For later original sketches, I want to test a concrete action before explaining a message about the community. If the song becomes an abstract speech of encouragement, this encounter is a reason to reconsider it.
