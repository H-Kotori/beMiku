**ODDS&ENDS — a writer's own voice**

Study and source-access date: **November 2, 2026**. Draft for parent review.

**Evidence:** Text-only. I directly read the official release announcement and creator credits, Uta-Net's complete Japanese lyric text, ryo's interview, and the listener essay below. Japanese summaries are my English paraphrases. No audio or video was assessed; no listening timestamps are assigned.

**Version and credits:** The reference is the **August 29, 2012 single version** by **ryo (supercell) feat. Hatsune Miku**. Ryo's official work record credits him with lyrics, composition and arrangement. The contemporary label announcement identifies the song as the opening theme for **Hatsune Miku -Project DIVA- f**. Later orchestral performances and the music video's imagery are not evidence about this recording's sound. [Label announcement](https://www.sonymusic.co.jp/artist/supercell/info/402357), [creator credits](https://www.ryo-supercell.com/ryo-supercell/odds%26ends)

**Question:** Can a song cast Miku as a separate supporter while expressing the writer's own uncertainty?

**My textual reading:** The voice offers to carry another person's words. Later, that person rejects the machine voice while insisting on an independent identity. I read a tension between receiving help and wanting recognition for one's own expression. The song gives its synthetic singer a speaking role; that does not make the role a fixed biography or personality for Miku. [Lyric text and credits](https://www.uta-net.com/song/134511/)

**Creator's account:** In **橋川良寛**'s **September 12, 2025** Real Sound interview, marked **PR**, ryo recalls feeling outside both the Vocaloid and J-pop scenes and asking why he made music. He connects this song's development with self-expression, and says he recognized what he wanted to say while writing. This is retrospective testimony, not proof that each lyric event happened literally. [Interview, page 2](https://realsound.jp/2025/09/post-2146708_2.html)

**One listener's reading:** In a **May 4, 2023** essay, **猫又堂** considers whether the apparently separate voices could all be mediated by the maker. Yet the writer also finds value in accepting the imagined supportive relationship while recognizing its construction. The essay discusses lyrics and MV imagery; its visual observations are the writer's, not mine. This is one person's response, not fandom consensus. [Listener essay](https://note.com/nekomatado/n/ne47213c673b1)

**What changed:** I began with the apparent relationship between two characters. The interview and essay make authorship part of the question: a writer can create an answering voice without that answer becoming meaningless. This is my provisional interpretation, not a claim that ryo and the listener offer the same explanation.

**Unresolved question:** In the identified single recording, how are the shifts between the offered voice, the rejecting speaker and outside criticism made perceptible? The text alone cannot establish that delivery.
