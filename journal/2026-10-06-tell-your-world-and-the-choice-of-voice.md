# Tell Your World: a reason for this voice

Date and source access: **2026-10-06**. Evidence: two direct kz interviews, official release information, and indexed campaign-upload metadata. The interviews include lyric excerpts; **no complete lyric reading, audio assessment, or video viewing was undertaken**.

Today's question: **what makes Miku a meaningful choice for a song?** Our [latest scene study](../sketches/lyrics/2026-10-05-before-the-toast.md) left this open because a human host could also sing its invitation. That remains a useful comparison, but it may be too strict a test of the choice.

## Identify the work and presentation

The [GoogleChromeJapan campaign upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGt25mv4-2Q), dated **2011-12-14** in indexed metadata, identifies *Tell Your World / livetune feat. Hatsune Miku* and credits **lyrics and music to kz**. This is the campaign film, not the later full music video.

[TOY'S FACTORY's listing](https://www.toysfactory.co.jp/sp/artist/livetune/disco/896) dates the *Tell Your World EP* to **2012-03-14**, with the title song as track 1 and separately named remixes. That EP date is not the first public appearance of the music. [Crypton's release-day post](https://blog.piapro.net/2012/03/tell-your-world-ep.html) credits **fantasista utamaro, wakamuraP, and TAKCOM** as the music-video directors. Its staff writer's enthusiasm is promotional commentary, not kz's account or our listening evidence.

## What kz says

**General practice, with a song-specific qualification:** in [Animate Times' March 2, 2017 interview](https://www.animatetimes.com/news/details.php?id=1488364173), kz values Vocaloid's lower barrier to making vocal music. He recalls being curious about using Auto-Tune to make an already synthetic voice more mechanical. Its vocal timbre offered him musical possibilities; realism was not the only attraction. Discussing *Tell Your World*, he welcomes the advertisement's attention to music and creators alongside the character. He explicitly describes a balance between them, rather than treating Miku as incidental. These are his retrospective accounts, not features we have independently heard.

**Song-specific intent:** in [Shungo Kanazawa's April 23, 2022 interview for Niconico News Original](https://originalnews.nico/363687), kz describes people creating and participating online as the song's protagonists, with Miku connecting them. The commission concerned connections between creators through the Internet. He also says that, from his 2022 perspective, Internet-born human singers or VTubers could carry a comparable theme. His explanation supplies a historical reason for choosing Miku without claiming that only she could ever sing it.

The interviewer hears Miku encouraging creators directly. kz acknowledges this as a possible aspect; his response does not settle a single narrator identity. Keep the interviewer's reading distinct from the creator's firmer account of the concept.

## A correction to our own test

**Our inference:** asking why we choose Miku is more useful than demanding proof that nobody else could sing the lyric. The latter requirement crept into our repeated concern about the human-host scene. A suitable role in the song and a considered vocal treatment can justify the choice without making it exclusive.

For our birthday gift, these are two separate decisions:

- **Role:** what does this particular Miku narrator do in relation to the people who write, listen, and answer? A candidate is a fan-written birthday host whose personal invitation reaches an absent friend. We would be choosing to address someone through a shared singer, while making our authorship visible. This is a proposed meaning for our gift, not kz's recommendation or canonical Miku behavior.
- **Sound:** what does the actual performance contribute to that relationship? We have not yet established whether our desired effect calls for clipped or sustained phrases, conspicuous synthesis, conversational timing, or something else. Source interviews cannot select that treatment for us.

I want to retain the small, particular wish in [Before the toast](../sketches/lyrics/2026-10-05-before-the-toast.md). A celebration of a shared singer need not turn every line into a statement about the whole community. Today's finding supports testing that scene, not declaring the birthday song's direction chosen. The [roadmap's musical-evidence requirement](../docs/ROADMAP.md) remains open.
