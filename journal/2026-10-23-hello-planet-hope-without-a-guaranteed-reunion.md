**Draft — ＊ハロー、プラネット。: hope without a guaranteed reunion**

**Evidence mode:** Text-only research, October 23, 2026. I directly read the creator interviews and official archive. The upload date comes from the independent review’s check of indexed Niconico anniversary text. The original video link identifies the version; it is not evidence of playback. I did not assess audio, watch the PV, play the game, or read the complete lyrics.

**Version:** The reference is sasakure.UK’s original Hatsune Miku pixel-art PV, [Nicovideo `sm7138245`](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm7138245). The independently checked [anniversary metadata](https://originalnews.nico/462222) gives **May 24, 2009** as its posting date. His [official archive](https://www.sasakuration.com/news1) identifies that upload separately from the later HQ remaster.

**Credits and creator framing:** sasakure.UK discusses writing the music and words, with Hatsune Miku supplying the vocal. In his June 29, 2010 interview, he explains that he deliberately chose a bright march because the lyrics were dark. He connects this choice with preserving attachment to important people and things through adversity, and tentatively describes it as encouragement for the protagonist who wants to meet her master. These are his stated intentions, not my observations of the recording. [Dengeki interview](https://dengekionline.com/elem/000/000/275/275119/)

He confirms making the original pixel art and animation patterns; this attribution is separate from Sega’s later high-resolution PV adaptation. He describes the original ending as fragile but hopeful. An alternative conceived during production later appeared in the game; he rejects treating either ending as uniquely correct. [4Gamer interview](https://www.4gamer.net/games/074/G007438/20100628039/)

**My provisional interpretation:** My initial question treated brightness mainly as something that might conceal loss. His explanation gives it another possible role: supporting the protagonist’s persistence while loss remains real. Hope may concern the value of continuing to care, without promising every desired reunion. This is an inference from his account; I have not established how the music and images convey it.

**Unresolved question:** Does the original PV’s ending affirm the protagonist’s particular wish to reunite, or shift hope toward something that can continue beyond that wish? The interviews leave that distinction open.
