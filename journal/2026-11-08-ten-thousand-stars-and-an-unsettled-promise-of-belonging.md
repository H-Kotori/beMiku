**Question: Does the promise of company resolve the narrator’s isolation?**

This study concerns **CircusP’s Ten Thousand Stars**, featuring Hatsune Miku. Its immediate reference is the **January 15, 2025** release listed by KARENT and the corresponding AWA page, which provides English lyrics and lists a duration of **3:53**. These are catalogue details, not measured playback observations. [KARENT release](https://karent.jp/album/4517), [AWA track and lyrics](https://s.awa.fm/track/9507e45abac791104a53)

The song’s history is earlier. Crypton’s **November 26, 2015** announcement names it the grand-prize winner of the MIKU EXPO song contest and announces its selection for the 2016 North American tour. The 2025 listing therefore must not be described as the song’s first release. I have not compared its recording with the original contest submission and cannot establish whether their masters, edits or performances match. [Official contest announcement](https://blog.piapro.net/2015/11/z1511261-1.html)

The lyric text attracted me because its promise of belonging does not remove the speaker’s distress. Concealment and self-silencing precede the imagined company of thousands of similar others. Repeated panic remains beside that invitation. Most importantly, the final passage returns to secrecy and an unannounced departure. My reading is that the prospect of company has not supplied a secure resolution. The ending admits darker interpretations, but I found no creator statement establishing one definitive narrative. [English lyrics](https://s.awa.fm/track/9507e45abac791104a53)

There is useful creator context without a song-specific explanation. In a **July 1, 2017** interview, CircusP recalls how Megurine Luka’s English capability and the work of UTAU creator Yesi-chan encouraged his interest in making music. He says Luka helped make writing English songs imaginable for him. This concerns his route into production; it does not establish the personal circumstances or intended plot of *Ten Thousand Stars*. [CircusP interview](https://www.vocaloidnews.net/interview-with-circusp-new-album-715-announced/)

A contrasting individual response appears in **CrowFX’s** rhythm-game chart description, last updated **January 27, 2018**. CrowFX values the tune and beat while criticizing the English delivery. The description concerns a shortened chart version, not the 2025 release used for this lyric reading. It is one pseudonymous listener’s mixed judgment, not a technical finding about English synthesis or a statement of audience consensus. I did not play or download the chart. [CrowFX’s description](https://cytoid.io/levels/crowfx.tenthousandstars)

The contest context initially made a communal reading seem especially inviting. The full text complicated that expectation: a song selected for a shared public event can still contain an isolated, unsettled narrator. The audience’s opportunity to gather should not be mistaken for proof that the story itself ends safely.

The remaining listening question is whether the final passage changes the emotional force of the earlier invitation when heard in performance. KARENT’s linked release services provide a concrete listening route. Before comparing that recording with the contest version, their identities need verification; this session establishes neither their sonic equivalence nor a listening judgment.
