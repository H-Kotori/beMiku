**ROKI — the target of its criticism**

Date: November 9, 2026, Asia/Shanghai  
Status: Reviewed/accepted text-only study  
Evidence: Text-only research. No audio or video assessed. All linked sources accessed November 9, 2026.

**Question: Who is being challenged by the song’s harsh words?**

The work is **ロキ / ROKI**, written and composed by **mikitoP**. Its vocals combine **Kagamine Rin and mikitoP himself**. UtaTen’s release field gives **February 27, 2018**; a February 28 release report confirms the human-and-synthetic vocal pairing. [Credits and Japanese lyrics](https://utaten.com/lyric/sa18090519/), [Contemporary release report](https://www.cdjournal.com/news/mikito-p/78691)

The [original YouTube reference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xg-qfsKN2_E) returned only minimal page text, and the [Niconico reference](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm32798041) was unavailable. Neither was played. The later **July 23, 2025** vinyl release includes separately named remixes by **Hiiragi Magnetite and emon(Tes.)**; these are outside this study, and no comparison of masters was made. [Label catalogue](https://www.ponycanyon.co.jp/music/PCJA000000185)

The printed lyrics initially suggested an attack on other musicians: they challenge self-conscious poses, follower identities, popularity and excuses. Yet the demand to keep going repeatedly includes both speaker and addressee. My interpretation is that the speaker’s position matters as much as the insults. Someone standing alongside another struggling creator can use the same forceful language differently from someone delivering judgment from a safe distance. This does not mean every listener must find that language encouraging. [Japanese lyrics](https://utaten.com/lyric/sa18090519/)

mikitoP supplies a specific explanation in a **November 28, 2018** interview. He describes wanting to encourage fellow producers and singers to survive and continue, speaking beside people he regards as comrades. Some of the sharper words address his own earlier self; he says the song is not intended to disparage a particular person. [Creator’s explanation](https://realsound.jp/2018/11/post-284248_2.html)

That interview also preserves a useful disagreement. The interviewer reads *ROKI* as a possible response to Hachi’s *DUNE*. mikitoP welcomes the interpretation but explicitly denies a connection. The distinction matters: a listener can notice a meaningful relationship between works without establishing that one creator intended to answer the other. [Interpretation and correction](https://realsound.jp/2018/11/post-284248_2.html)

A different individual response comes from collaborator **ろこる**, who recalls being strongly excited by the song on first hearing it. That is their reported response, not my judgment of its sound. [Collaborator’s statement](https://www.cdjournal.com/news/mikito-p/78691)

My reading changed from identifying an external target to considering self-criticism and solidarity within the same address. The creator’s explanation helped locate that position without removing the text’s abrasiveness.

The remaining listening question concerns the duet: how do Rin’s and mikitoP’s deliveries distribute challenge, humour and mutual encouragement? The lyric page alone cannot establish the vocal exchanges or their effect.
