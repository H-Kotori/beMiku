**砂の惑星 / DUNE — homage without guaranteed agreement**

Study and source-access date: **November 1, 2026**. Draft for parent review.

**Continuity:** DUNE was already studied on September 27. Today’s explicit selection is a focused follow-up on homage and originality, extending the earlier anniversary discussion without repeating its comparison of creators’ positions on the scene.

**Evidence:** Text-only. I directly read the official event credits, artist release pages, and J-WAVE’s written report of a radio appearance. I did not hear that broadcast, assess either song recording or video, read complete lyrics, or access the individual comments discussed by J-WAVE. No listening timestamps are assigned.

**Version and credits:** The reference remains the [Miku MV, `AS4q9yaWJkI`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AS4q9yaWJkI), announced **July 21, 2017**: **Hachi** wrote, composed, and arranged it; **Hatsune Miku** is the featured vocalist; **Minakata Laboratory** made the video. [Official credits](https://magicalmirai.com/2017/), [MV announcement](https://reissuerecords.net/2017/07/21/ハチ『砂の惑星』mv公開映像制作は「南方研究所/)

The artist’s *BOOTLEG* page separately identifies **砂の惑星（＋初音ミク）** as a self-cover on the album released **November 1, 2017**. That is a distinct version, not the selected Miku MV. [Album record](https://reissuerecords.net/discography/bootleg/)

**Question:** Does referring to a shared musical past guarantee that the gesture will be welcomed?

**Reported account:** J-WAVE’s **November 10, 2017** article describes titles and lyrics from influential Vocaloid songs appearing as homage in DUNE. It reports objections from some Niconico users to reusing others’ work. Yonezu’s quoted response connects challenges to originality with his choice of the album title *BOOTLEG*. The article does not identify those commenters or establish how many there were. [Broadcaster’s report](https://news.j-wave.co.jp/2017/11/post-4505.html)

**What changed:** I initially wondered whether these references might be unfamiliar to newer listeners. This evidence cannot establish anyone’s experience or familiarity. It instead makes me separate recognizing a reference from approving its use: those are different questions. The reported objections do not settle the song’s meaning or represent its whole audience.

**Unresolved question:** What did individual listeners recognize in the allusions, and why did that recognition lead to appreciation or objection? Their own attributed accounts would be needed.
