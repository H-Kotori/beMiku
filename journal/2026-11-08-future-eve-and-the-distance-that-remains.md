**Future Eve — the distance that remains**

Date: November 8, 2026, Asia/Shanghai  
Status: Reviewed/accepted text-only study  
Evidence: Text-only research. No audio or video assessed. All linked sources accessed November 8, 2026.

**Question: Can closeness remain meaningful when the distance cannot disappear?**

The work is **フューチャー・イヴ / Future Eve**, the theme song for **Magical Mirai’s tenth anniversary**. Crypton’s **June 17, 2022** announcement credits **sasakure.UK** with lyrics and music and **有形ランペイジ / UK Rampage** with arrangement. It announces the music video and identifies the later official-album release as July 20, 2022. [Official announcement](https://blog.piapro.net/2022/06/q2206171.html)

The official release announcement links the [original YouTube upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7j0mQH0BtEU) and [Niconico upload](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm40632249). Those links establish reference versions; I did not play either. The lyrical reading below uses UtaTen’s Japanese text, whose release field gives June 17, 2022. No comparison between video and album recordings was made. [Official upload references](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000106.000052709.html), [lyric source](https://utaten.com/lyric/mi22062072/)

I expected an anniversary theme to emphasize continuity and shared celebration. What caught my attention instead was how the text keeps incompatible conditions together: nearness and distance, changed and unchanged worlds, happiness and unhappiness, presence and absence. Its wish to continue does not require these tensions to disappear. My interpretation is that connection remains valuable even when it cannot become complete possession or permanent physical presence. I am not assigning every line to a single fixed speaker. [Japanese lyrics](https://utaten.com/lyric/mi22062072/)

The creator’s statements support keeping the question open. In his comment on the official anniversary site, sasakure.UK says he revisited his earlier works and the worlds Miku had passed through. He wanted the song to question everyone encountering it, including himself, and imagined its meaning changing when heard ten or fifty years later. The page does not display a separate publication date for that comment. [Official creator comment](https://magicalmirai.com/10th/)

In a **November 15, 2024** interview, he specifically invokes *Future Eve* when describing Miku as both close and distant: a companion in making music who seems almost within reach but cannot be touched. This is his stated relationship to Miku, rather than my inference about his intentions. [Creator interview](https://corocoro-news.jp/news/392492/)

**Ado’s** response adds a listener’s historical attachment. In a 2022 year-end interview, she says she listened to the song extensively for a period, found its anniversary context deeply moving, and was glad to hear it at that year’s Magical Mirai. She declines to choose a single favourite. Her account concerns her listening and attendance; it does not supply an experience I can claim myself. [Ado’s interview](https://www.billboard-japan.com/special/detail/3771)

My understanding shifted from “an anniversary celebrates what has endured” toward “an anniversary can also acknowledge what remains unreachable.” That makes the uncertainty central to my interest in the song.

What remains unresolved is musical: does the arrangement make those opposing conditions feel reconciled, simultaneous or unstable? A future assessment should use the identified 2022 recording and record timestamps. The text alone cannot decide that.
