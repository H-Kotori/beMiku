**Deep Sea Girl — the risk of being seen**

Session label: **November 4, 2026**. Research and source access: **November 5, 2026 (UTC+08; November 4, 2026 UTC)**. Reviewed/accepted text-only study.

**Evidence:** Text-only. I directly read Nippon Crown’s release record, UtaTen’s complete Japanese lyrics, the smileY inc. interview and TuneCore’s later-version record. The original Nico upload was inaccessible; its September 1 date was available in indexed text of Nico’s anniversary report. No audio or video was assessed; no listening timestamps are assigned. Japanese summaries are my English paraphrases.

**Version and credits:** The reference is **track 7 of 四季彩の星**, released **September 15, 2010**, featuring **Hatsune Miku**, with lyrics, composition and arrangement by **ゆうゆ (yuuyu)**. Nippon Crown identifies it as a song written for that album. [Label record](https://www.crownrecord.co.jp/s/c01/artist/yuyu/discography?ct=jpop&ima=0000&link=ROBO004)

[The original Nico upload](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm11956364) dates to **September 1, 2010**, according to [Nico’s anniversary report — indexed text only](https://originalnews.nico/469477). The **December 16, 2022** release, *深海少女 -deep sea girl-*, is explicitly described as a rearrangement. These dates identify separate releases; I have not compared their recordings. [Later-version record](https://linkco.re/GpfPu601?lang=ja)

**Question:** Why can the wish to connect coexist with a renewed impulse to hide?

**My textual reading:** Interest in another person does not immediately end the speaker’s withdrawal. Being noticed exposes her shame about how she appears, so attraction and retreat coexist. Later, reaching out and being helped both enter the action. I read hope as emerging through vulnerability and contact; the narrative does not show that affection automatically removes someone’s distress. [Lyrics](https://utaten.com/lyric/jb71009101/)

**An attributed response and creator context:** In a **May 6, 2015** interview about smileY inc.’s separate cover, singer **大坪由佳 (Yuka Otsubo)** says she values how the song includes negative human feelings while allowing hope to appear. She describes a personally cleansing effect. This is her account as a listener and later performer, not a universal effect or my listening observation.

Later in that interview, while discussing another song and his writing more generally, yuuyu says uninterrupted positivity would feel unconvincing coming from him. That helps contextualize his approach, but it is not a song-specific explanation of every image in *Deep Sea Girl*. [Interview](https://www.animatetimes.com/news/details.php?id=1430379648)

**What changed:** A simple description of rescue would overlook the hesitation that precedes contact. Otsubo’s response draws my attention to the place difficult feelings retain within a hopeful reading.

**Unresolved question:** How does the 2010 recording convey the difference between wanting to be found and fearing exposure? The lyric sequence gives a question for listening, not evidence of the vocal delivery.
