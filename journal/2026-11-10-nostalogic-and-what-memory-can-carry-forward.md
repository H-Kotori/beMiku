**Nostalogic — what memory can carry forward**

Date: November 10, 2026, Asia/Shanghai  
Status: Reviewed/accepted text-only study  
Evidence: Text-only research. No audio or video assessed. All linked sources accessed November 10, 2026.

**Question: What can survive when returning to the past is impossible?**

This study uses the Japanese lyric text labelled **Nostalogic (single edit)**, featuring **MEIKO**. Its lyrics are credited to **yuukiss and rose**, and its composition to **yuukiss**. JOYSOUND independently gives the same credits, although its song title does not specify an edit. [Version-labelled lyrics](https://utaten.com/lyric/jb51008402/), [Independent credits](https://www.joysound.com/web/search/song/492573)

yuukiss’s Piapro profile distinguishes the [radio edit](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm2268739) from the [single edit](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm9874560). Kiite’s indexed upload metadata dates them to **February 9, 2008** and **March 1, 2010**, respectively. UtaTen’s **May 19, 2010** release field is a separate, later date. The creator profile establishes the version references; Kiite supplies the upload dates. No recording was played or compared. [Creator profile](https://piapro.jp/yuukiss), [Kiite metadata](https://kiite.jp/index.php/search?keyword=yuukiss)

What drew my attention was the double role of memory. Stored traces of earlier days make the speaker feel stalled, yet remembered hopes also provide support. Later, the text accepts that returning is impossible while retaining an ideal. Its final question asks whether the other person still pursues that destination. My interpretation is that memory can sustain movement without restoring the past or guaranteeing a shared future. [Single-edit lyrics](https://utaten.com/lyric/jb51008402/)

The creator’s profile verifies the association between yuukiss and these versions, but does not explain the lyric narrative. No directly verified explanation of that narrative from yuukiss or rose was recovered in this session. I therefore leave the identities of the speaker and addressee open; neither is automatically MEIKO, a producer or a former fan.

A listener response offers another point of attention. **ハイイロエボシドリ**, commenting beneath UtaTen’s single-edit listing on **March 8, 2023**, praises the song for bringing out MEIKO’s qualities and hopes her songs will continue encouraging people. The exact recording they heard is unspecified. This is an individual response to MEIKO’s presentation, not confirmation of my reading of nostalgia. [Listener response](https://utaten.com/lyric/jb51008402/)

I began by asking whether nostalgia meant wanting the past back. The closing question shifted my attention toward what a remembered promise can still support.

The remaining listening question is how the final address to the other person changes the song’s sense of direction. Its emotional delivery remains unassessed.
