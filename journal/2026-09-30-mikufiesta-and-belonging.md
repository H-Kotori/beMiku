# MikuFiesta: an invitation with a place behind it

Date and source access: **2026-09-30**. Evidence: official event and release records, the creator's blog, and one public reader comment. **No audio or video was assessed, and no complete lyric text was read in this session.** Spanish and Japanese prose is summarized in English.

Today's question: **how can a creator welcome people through Miku without making their own musical background disappear?** This follows the [Double Lariat encounter](2026-09-29-double-lariat-and-permission.md), where another creator's example made expression feel possible.

## Identify the work before interpreting it

**MikuFiesta — AlexTrip Sands, featuring Hatsune Miku.** The [official fifth-anniversary contest result](https://mikuexpo.com/5thcontest/) names it the grand-prize winner. Crypton's [November 19, 2019 announcement](https://blog.piapro.net/2019/11/fm1911191-1.html) identifies a Spanish-language song, also mentioning expressions of affection and encouragement in several languages. That is the organizer's description, not a transcription or our own pronunciation assessment.

The [official contest video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TMdHGvrwyJc), linked by the event, is the reference for a future session. Its audio was not retrieved. The later [KARENT single](https://karent.jp/album/4096) lists a **2024-04-17** release and credits **Pimienta Kast** for artwork. This is a catalog release date, not the song's original publication date. No identity between the contest recording, later music video, and distributed master has been established here.

## The creator's account

In his [profile](https://divasensalsa.blogspot.com/p/acerca-de.html), AlexTrip Sands describes growing up musically with Venezuelan traditions and connects his enthusiasm for salsa and merengue with Caracas. This is a particular musician's background, not a description of all Spanish-speaking creators.

His [November 19, 2019 post](https://divasensalsa.blogspot.com/2019/11/lo-logramos.html) says the tour's celebration, including Spain, helped shape *MikuFiesta*. He aimed for an accessible introduction to Miku, combining Latin, dance-music and flamenco elements, and credits Pimienta Kast's artwork. These are creator statements, not findings from listening.

He also recalls hostility toward earlier work, while saying support was greater, and urges creators to take risks without abandoning their styles. This is personal testimony, not a survey.

**One listener's response:** commenter MangaKD (July 30, 2020) reports surprise at hearing Spanish in a London concert video. That individual recollection does not verify the performance or establish a consensus.

## Inclusion beyond the song

In [a February 5, 2020 post](https://divasensalsa.blogspot.com/2020/02/me-llevaron-la-mikufiesta-en-un-bolsillo.html), AlexTrip Sands says logistics kept him and Pimienta Kast from attending the European tour. Fans in Barcelona included him through messages, pictures, and clips, extending the exchange through the queue, show, and dinner afterward. He describes feeling brought along from Venezuela. We read his public retelling; we did not access the private exchanges or watch those clips.

**Our inference:** making Miku feel local can be a way of welcoming others, rather than a barrier they must first overcome. The creator's account also makes belonging practical: people include someone who cannot be physically present. Neither point requires treating Spanish as an exotic feature or assigning Miku one national identity.
