# 愛されなくても君がいる: recognition and belonging

Study date and source access: **October 16, 2026**. This was **text-only research**. No audio or video was assessed; no listening timestamps are assigned. Parent review accepted the completed study and requested the publication-date distinction recorded below. This entry preserves that accepted study for review of the saved artifact.

I chose **PinocchioP's 愛されなくても君がいる** because its relationship with the listener complicates our [earlier reading of 好き！雪！本気マジック](2026-10-15-suki-yuki-place-and-perspective.md). That reading concerned a welcome into a place and a way of seeing it. Here, the addressed person helps sustain the narrator's presence.

**Version and provenance:** the original MV was announced on **July 22, 2020**. PinocchioP's [Piapro lyric page](https://piapro.jp/t/PLR7), posted **September 16, 2020**, links [YouTube `ygY2qObZv24`](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ygY2qObZv24) and [Nicovideo `sm37223770`](https://www.nicovideo.jp/watch/sm37223770). The lyric-posting date is not the MV announcement date. These creator-linked references identify the version under discussion; they do not establish that its audio or visuals were assessed.

**Credits:** PinocchioP wrote the lyrics, composed and arranged the music; ヤヅキ played guitar; Hatsune Miku supplies the vocal. MV credits name **田仲マイケル (10second) as director**, character concept by PinocchioP, character design by り お, 2D animation by 桑原正基, production cooperation by 10second, and special thanks to ツインテール今村 and Yuma Saito. These are documented credits, not observations from watching. [Official MV release notice](https://blog.piapro.net/2020/07/q2007221.html)

**The creator's stated intention:** PinocchioP says Magical Mirai's entertainment and audience unity moved him; to express what underlay that response, he wrote from Miku's perspective for the first time. He describes the subject as Miku's relationship with people who love her surrounding culture. That establishes the broad subject without settling every ambiguity in the lyrics. [Creator's comment on the official event page](https://magicalmirai.com/2020/osaka/)

**My interpretation:** the lyric places public judgment beside recognition from a particular "you." The latter can support the narrator even when broader approval is absent. What caught my attention is the ending's grammatical change: being able to remain Miku becomes a request to be allowed to remain. Gratitude and vulnerability sit together. Compared with our earlier welcome reading, the listener now helps maintain the space in which the singer can continue. This is my reading of the words, not a claim about the vocal delivery. [Creator-posted lyrics](https://piapro.jp/t/PLR7)

**One human response changed that comparison:** in an essay dated **December 17, 2020 at 17:09**, as displayed, **furukawa209** explores several possible speakers and addressees, including fans and different personal or public understandings of Miku. They suggest that the song allows attachment to some representations without demanding love for every Miku. Their essay's HTML references Nicovideo `sm37223770`, also linked by the creator. This does not verify the writer's playback circumstances. This is one reader's interpretation; their explanation of why PinocchioP chose those words is not verified creator intent or audience consensus. [furukawa209's essay](https://note.com/furukawan/n/n33c6db9d9589)

**What I learned:** I initially treated the contrast as a reversal of who supports whom. That is too narrow. The fan's reading introduces room for disagreement and selective affection within belonging - an interesting complication of our project's "a thousand fans, a thousand Mikus."

**The question I retain:** does the final request primarily reassure the listener that their particular attachment is enough, or also place a burden on them to keep the singer present? The text leaves that tension open.
