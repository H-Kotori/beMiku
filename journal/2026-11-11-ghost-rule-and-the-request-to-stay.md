**Ghost Rule — the request to stay**

Date: November 11, 2026, Asia/Shanghai  
Status: Reviewed and accepted — text-only study; unpublished  
Evidence: Text-only research. No audio or video assessed. Source access and retrieval attempts dated November 11, 2026.

**Question: Does asking to be judged also ask the other person to remain?**

The work is **ゴーストルール / Ghost Rule**, written and composed by **DECO*27**, featuring **Hatsune Miku**. Yutura’s preserved metadata dates the [original YouTube upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KushW6zvazM) to **January 8, 2016**, and reproduces credits for arranger **Naoki Itai** and illustrator **八三**. Direct YouTube retrieval failed. Uta-Net’s **September 28, 2016** release field concerns a later catalogue entry, not the original upload date. [Upload metadata](https://yutura.net/channel/17843/popular/KushW6zvazM/), [Songwriting credits and lyrics](https://www.uta-net.com/song/215591/)

The Japanese lyrics place demands for exposure and judgment beside requests for love and continued contact. The speaker appears to release the other person from an embrace, yet keeps addressing them. My interpretation is that admitting wrongdoing has not ended the need for attachment. Asking someone to judge can still keep them involved. The text does not establish forgiveness, reconciliation or a successful change in behaviour. [Japanese lyrics](https://www.uta-net.com/song/215591/)

DECO*27 gives a more definite account of the narrative in a **February 28, 2017** interview. He describes a protagonist who builds further lies around an initial lie and is eventually undermined by it. News stories supplied cautionary examples that prompted the song. This is his stated story and inspiration; my reading of the repeated appeals concerns how that story addresses another person. It does not replace his explanation with an innocent-victim narrative. [Creator interview](https://www.animatetimes.com/news/details.php?id=1488164517)

A different response appears in Crypton’s **September 28, 2016** album announcement. Staff writer **うさ太** says hearing the song brings “Magical” to mind and prompts singing along. This is an individual response inside promotional writing, not independent evidence of audience consensus. It shows one listener connecting the song with participation, rather than only with its protagonist’s predicament. [Attributed response](https://blog.piapro.net/2016/09/p1609281-1.html)

The study changed the emphasis from identifying a confession to examining what confession asks of its recipient. The first-person words also need not be DECO*27’s autobiography or Miku describing herself: the creator explicitly describes a constructed protagonist.

The remaining listening question is how the requests for judgment and affection differ in delivery. Whether they sound defiant, pleading, evasive or unstable cannot be settled from the printed words. The identified original upload remains a reference for a future listening session.
