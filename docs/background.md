# Learning who Miku can be

Sources checked: 2026-09-25. These are starting notes, not a definition of what every fan's Miku must be.

## Where this begins

Crypton Future Media released Hatsune Miku on August 31, 2007, as singing synthesis software powered by Yamaha's VOCALOID technology. August 31, 2027 therefore marks twenty years since that release. Crypton's 2008 account already describes music, illustrations, animation tools, remixes, and fan events growing around her. It also describes Piapro as a place for creators to collaborate and exchange ideas. That document is a historical snapshot, not a source for present-day audience totals. [Crypton's 2008 introduction](https://ec.crypton.co.jp/download/pdf/info_miku_e.pdf)

The anniversary measures elapsed time since the software's debut; Miku's official character profile still gives her age as 16. “Piapro” derives from “peer production,” expressing the idea that people with different skills create something together. [Official character profiles](https://piapro.net/intl/en_character.html)

## Three things to keep separate

**The character** supplies an identity that can appear in images, stories, and performances. **The software** produces a singing voice from musical instructions. **The producer and collaborators** make the particular work we encounter. Crypton's original product page credits Saki Fujita for voice material and KEI for illustration; it describes entering notes and lyrics, then editing dynamics, timbre, and vibrato. [Original product documentation](https://ec.crypton.co.jp/pages/prod/virtualsinger/cv01)

For this project, that distinction is a reading discipline: identify a song's actual credits, ask whose perspective its narrator carries, and avoid turning one interpretation into universal character lore. A song can teach us about its creator's Miku without settling what Miku means to everyone.

## The six Piapro Characters

This reference records initial releases and credited voice artists. MEIKO and KAITO predate Miku; Rin and Len debuted together, while Luka's debut supported Japanese and English singing. The official English spelling is **Kagamine Len**. [Official profiles and release dates](https://piapro.net/intl/en_character.html)

## Questions to carry into listening

- When does Miku function as a fictional narrator, a performer addressing listeners, or the subject of a song?
- Which choices make two producers' voices recognizably different: tuning, register, phrasing, arrangement, or lyrical viewpoint?
- What might our anniversary song offer both longtime listeners and someone encountering Miku for the first time?

These questions remain open. Future notes should distinguish documented facts, direct listening observations, and our interpretations, and should preserve disagreement rather than manufacture a single fan consensus.
