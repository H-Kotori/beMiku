# A cup with your name — lyric exercise 01

Date: **2026-10-02**. Version: **0.1**. Original text drafted by Codex for beMiku. **Words only: no melody, MIDI, recording, or Miku vocal render.** Japanese is the working language of this exercise; the final song language remains open. English glosses explain the sense rather than supplying singable translations.

## Keep the action fixed

A singer-host writes an absent friend's name on a paper cup at a birthday gathering. The friend is alive, elsewhere at their own desk, and unable to attend. We imagine the host as one possible fictional Miku narrator. Neither version speaks for every fan or establishes character lore. The paper material and birthday belong to this scene brief; the fragments themselves do not establish them yet.

The [MikuFiesta study](../../journal/2026-09-30-mikufiesta-and-belonging.md) prompted a concrete act of inclusion. The [Hiyimi reading](../../journal/2026-10-01-the-sound-of-me-and-confidence.md) challenged our preference for hesitation. Those are conceptual influences; the scene and wording below were newly drafted, without adapting either song's lyrics or melody.

## A — tentative

> 君の名前を<br>
> コップに書いた<br>
> 遠くへ乾杯<br>
> 届くといいな

**Sense:** I wrote your name on a cup. A toast toward you far away; I hope it reaches you.

## B — assured

> 君の名前を<br>
> コップに書いた<br>
> そっちの机も<br>
> 今夜は会場

**Sense:** I wrote your name on a cup. Your desk over there is tonight's venue too.

## What changed on the page?

| Question | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does the host do? | Writes the friend's name on a cup, then hopes the gesture reaches them. | Performs the same action, then declares the friend's space part of the gathering. |
| What room does the friend have? | No reply is demanded, but the friend stays outside the scene. | Their ordinary desk becomes part of the scene, but the host makes that decision for them. |
| Main revision risk | An absent person's named cup could suggest mourning without surrounding context. | An assured welcome could become pressure to participate. |

The intended contrast is in the last two lines. The common opening keeps both voices grounded in the same action. A is uncertain about the gesture reaching the friend; it does not establish an insecure personality. These are editorial readings of our own text, not findings about how the lines sound when sung. Editorial review changed A's directional particle from の to へ to make the toast point outward more clearly.

## Pronunciation preparation

Shared opening readings: **きみのなまえを / こっぷにかいた**. A continues **とおくへかんぱい / とどくといいな**; B continues **そっちのつくえも / こんやはかいじょう**. The particle へ is pronounced /e/ here.

Manual mora counts are **7 / 7 / 8 / 7** for A and **7 / 7 / 8 / 8** for B. The small っ counts; the combined じょ counts once. These counts prepare later phrasing work. They do not specify rhythm, pitch accent, note lengths, or successful voicebank pronunciation. The last-line difference must be handled explicitly if both versions are later compared over one melody.

## Provisional judgment and next revision

I prefer B's concrete move into the friend's room, while A better preserves their freedom not to answer. Keep both for now. Next revise only B's closing couplet so participation becomes an offer the friend can accept or decline; retain the named cup and the desk. Check whether the revised fragment still feels assured.

The text could currently be sung by a human host as well. It has not yet earned a specific reason to use Miku's voice, and no musical direction is selected. Later phrasing and listening must address that question. The [roadmap's listening requirement](../../docs/ROADMAP.md) remains open; this exercise does not substitute for it.
