# Before the toast — scene study 02

Date: **2026-10-05**. Version: **0.1**. Original text drafted by Codex for beMiku. **Words only; no melody, MIDI, audio assessment, or vocal render.** Japanese remains an exploratory language choice. The English gloss explains meaning rather than supplying a singable translation.

## A wish interrupts the welcome

In this fictional scene, the singer-host is being celebrated at a birthday gathering. An absent, living friend said earlier that they would still be working at their own desk. The host has written the friend's name on a paper cup and is composing a message before the toast. They want to hear the friend's familiar joke, then catch themselves asking at an inconvenient time.

The [ryo text study](../../journal/2026-10-04-kocchi-muite-baby-and-second-thoughts.md) prompted this experiment in self-interruption. The situation and wording here are newly drafted. There is no romantic premise or adopted melody. The friend and their work are invented scene details, not facts about a real listener or Miku's official character.

## Passage

The first four lines are new. The remaining four reproduce [exercise 01's C](2026-10-02-cup-with-your-name.md) unchanged, placing the existing invitation after the host's exposed wish.

> いつもの冗談<br>
> 今日も聞かせて<br>
> ……って、君はまだ<br>
> 仕事中だね
>
> 君の名前を<br>
> コップに書いた
>
> そっちの机で<br>
> よければ乾杯

**Sense:** Tell me one of your usual jokes today, too. …Wait, you're still at work. I wrote your name on a cup. If you'd like, raise a toast at your desk over there.

The host wants something ordinary and particular from this friend. Catching the request changes how they approach the friend; it need not erase that desire. The cup has already been prepared, and the final invitation leaves the response open. This is our intended scene, not evidence of how a sung performance would communicate it.

## Preparation and judgment

New-line readings: **いつものじょうだん / きょうもきかせて / って、きみはまだ / しごとちゅうだね**. Manual mora counts are **8 / 7 / 7 / 7**; C remains **7 / 7 / 8 / 8**. The combined じょ, きょ, and ちゅ each count once, their following う adds a mora, and the small っ counts. The ellipsis specifies a textual interruption only; no rest length or musical rhythm has been assigned.

A separate Japanese/editorial review checked the sense and counts. It moved まだ closer to 仕事中 for more conversational word order. The request is already a complete sentence; the interruption changes the train of thought rather than cutting a sentence short.

I prefer this as a scene experiment because the host now has a recognizable wish beyond being welcoming. The work reminder could still sound disappointed or reproachful when performed. The intended delivery is a realization about timing, but that has not been tested. The birthday and the friend's earlier message also remain in the scene brief rather than being fully established by the lyric alone.

Keep this passage provisional. It could still be spoken or sung by a human host; it does not yet supply a specific reason for Miku's voice. A useful next study should address that choice instead of repeatedly polishing the invitation.
